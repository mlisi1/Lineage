2. Lit or cosy chosen: cosy

Cosy was chosen on 2026-10-18 (ADR 0013) and is what every view now draws; lit stays here for comparison. Lit is the style settled in R0: a near-black outline, the far side of every mass in a cool shade, a lit back. Cosy is the proposal after Stardew Valley's animals, keeping the camera straight above: the same body plans, in richer colours; the outline is each animal's own colour taken nearly to black and toward plum instead of one grey-black; shade turns violet and light turns gold; eyes a size larger, with a glint from smaller sizes; and a soft shadow on the ground under each animal, away from the light. An animal that lives in the water is seen through it: its colours go toward the sea's blue and its outline toward deep water, so that it does not seem to walk on the sea. Each pair of pictures has the same animals walking the same paths.

Under water chosen: murk

An animal that lives in the water must look as if it were in it, not walking on it. The water where it swims decides how much: light in the shallows, dark blue in the deep. Three ways to show it, each in a table (every swimming specimen and an eel in four colours: out of the water, then in the shallows, at middle depth and in the deep, on that water's colour) and in a stretch of sea from the shore to the deep below.

  • Hue (the first try): every colour turns toward the water's hue at its own lightness, then into a haze of the water that thickens with depth. Some bodies look tinted rather than under water.
  • Murk (chosen, ADR 0013, and what every view draws): the animal keeps its own hues, darkened (15 % in the shallows to 45 % in the deep), then takes some of the water's colour (15 % to 50 %), as if the water showed through it. No cost.
  • Glass: its own hues, a little darkened (10 % to 30 %), and the whole body drawn see-through (80 % opaque in the shallows to 45 % in the deep), so that the sea itself, its bands and waves, shows through it. Each swimmer in sight is then drawn on its own and laid on the scene, which costs a little every frame: R5 would measure it.

Between sim ticks

The sim moves an animal every ten minutes, a fraction of a cell at a time; watched in real time that is stillness. Here each animal keeps to the place the sim last gave it and lives there: it walks to a new place when one comes (hurrying if far behind), and between times pauses, looks about, strolls a little within a leash of its place (0.4 cells and 0.6 of its length), and breathes, slower the heavier it is. Where it is drawn is the renderer's own; where it is, is the sim's. In this picture a stand-in for the sim moves every place on every 3 seconds instead of every ten minutes, so that the loop shows a few moves.

One body plan per species new

The kind an animal is drawn as changes at thresholds of its genes (two leg pairs a beast, one a hopper), so two animals of one species either side of a threshold looked like different kinds. Now each species is drawn as the kind of its average member (the species come from M6); within that plan each animal still shows its own genes: size, build, colour, markings. An animal takes its species' kind only where it lives where its species does, so that a fish is never drawn walking on the shore.
