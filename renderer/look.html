Lineage look sheet

How the renderer draws the world, and what there is to choose. World: …. Renderer build ….

The world is a continent, and the screen shows it from three distances. The map view holds all of it. The middle view is four times closer: a region, its woods and shores, animals as small bodies. The local view is four times closer again and is about one animal: it and what it walks through fill the screen. All three are painted from the same cells by the same rules, and the plants of the middle view stand in the same places in the local one, drawn larger and in more detail.

Art pixels are 2 screen pixels: every picture marked full size is drawn at 512×300 and shown at 1024×600, pixel for pixel what the 7″ screen will show. Everything is drawn by the renderer spike (R0); plants and animals are drawn by rule, which is what lets the same plant be drawn at two distances, and can be redrawn by hand once the look is fixed.

This page is live (drawn with …): the creatures walk. npm run look writes a copy to reports/R0-look-sheet/.

A copy to read anywhere, generated by npm run look in renderer/; do not edit by hand. The pictures with animals in them are short loops, and the animals jump back when a loop restarts. The live page (npm run dev there, then /look.html) runs without end.

1. The map view settled

The whole continent, 2 art pixels to the cell: heights and cover interpolated between cells, each animal a dot in its own colour (your M3).

2. The middle view settled

A sixteenth of the continent, 8 art pixels to the cell. As you chose: the ground interpolated and dithered (G4), the large plants (P3), animals as outlined body plans (C3). The animals are drawn a little smaller than before: 0.85 of the first size, so an animal of mass 1 is 7 art pixels long here.

3. The local view new

One animal, from close to the ground. Nothing is rescaled between the views: the animal is the same 0.85 of a cell long and the tree the same three cells tall as in the middle view. The camera is simply much closer, so the animal is 20 to 45 art pixels long instead of 7 (larger on the screen than your S3 was), and a grown tree stands taller than the animal is long, as it should.

What is drawn for this distance:

  • Ground: the same painter, with the patches of the middle view shown larger and given ragged edges, long blades, wet sand and a band of foam on the shore.
  • Plants: the trees and bushes of the middle view in the same places, drawn with many clumps of foliage, single leaves, bark and roots; grass as tufts in every cell that grows it, so an animal walks through it; flowers in grown grass; stones on bare ground.
  • Animals: outline, light, eyes with a glint, legs with an edge and feet, and the markings of section 4. They amble instead of hurrying.
  • What covers the animal goes see-through. A tree in front of the one the picture is about shows it through instead of hiding it.

What stands in the local view, enlarged twice: broadleaf grown and young, conifer grown and young, bush (plain, in berry) and young, grass grown and young, three flowers, two stones.

5. What it costs

The board is not on the desk, so this is an estimate: the three views measured on the development PC and scaled to the board's processor. An Allwinner H618 scores about 150 in Geekbench 6 on one core and this PC about 3,150: the board is some 21 times slower per core. It is another architecture and another graphics stack, so read the last column as an order of magnitude.

What follows from it:

  • Drawing the frames fits in all three views, with room. The local view is the cheapest per frame: few animals are in sight.
  • Painting ground is the one thing that does not fit in a frame. On the board a piece of the local view's ground would stop the picture for half a second, and the daily repaint of the other two for nine. Both have to be painted in the background (a worker), ahead of the camera for the local view. Measured as it is, the local view drops to 2 frames a second while a piece is painted.
  • Everything still hangs on the graphics driver. With the GPU the picture is small work; without it nothing here is fast enough. Reports for this board show the driver loading while Chromium still draws in software, so this is the first thing to try when the board is on the desk.
  • A 1 GB board is tight next to the simulation; 2 GB or more is safer.

"At the board's speed" is the same page with its processor throttled 21 times in Chromium; in brackets, the slowest frame in a hundred. The page's process and the memory are Chromium's own figures on the PC. Sources for the scores: Geekbench 6 uploads for the Orange Pi Zero 3 (same chip) and for the Core Ultra 9 275HX.

To choose, and to say

  • How close the local view is: L1, L2 or L3.
  • The local view's plants and ground: whether this is the amount of detail you had in mind, and what is missing.
  • The markings: which to keep, drop or add.
  • The size of the animals against plants, now that both are seen from close: 0.85 is one number for all three views.
