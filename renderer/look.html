Lineage look sheet

How the renderer draws the world, and what there is to choose. World: …. Renderer build ….

The world is a continent, and the screen shows it from three distances. The map view holds all of it. The middle view is four times closer: a region, its woods and shores, animals as small bodies. The local view is four times closer again and is about one animal: it and what it walks through fill the screen. All three are painted from the same cells by the same rules, and the plants of the middle view stand in the same places in the local one, drawn larger and in more detail.

Art pixels are 2 screen pixels: every picture marked full size is drawn at 512×300 and shown at 1024×600, pixel for pixel what the 7″ screen will show. Everything is drawn by the renderer spike (R0); plants and animals are drawn by rule, which is what lets the same plant be drawn at two distances, and can be redrawn by hand once the look is fixed.

A copy to read anywhere, generated by npm run look in renderer/; do not edit by hand. The pictures with animals in them are short loops, and the animals jump back when a loop restarts. The live page (npm run dev there, then /look.html) runs without end.

1. The map view settled

The whole continent, 2 art pixels to the cell: heights and cover interpolated between cells, each animal a dot in its own colour (your M3).

2. The middle view trees redrawn

A sixteenth of the continent, 8 art pixels to the cell. As you decided: animals stay at 0.5 here, larger than life (at the local view's size they would be three pixels long), and the vegetation is redrawn as T1: the same rule as the local view at a quarter of the size, so a tree is the same tree from both distances. With it come the stages of growth (a wood now has saplings at its edges where it used to have young trees everywhere) and shadows painted on the ground instead of drawn with each plant.

4. Vegetation: every variation new

What a plant looks like follows from three things the simulation knows: its form (grass, bush, broadleaf, conifer), how much of it there is on its cell (biomass), and the date. This section shows all of it: the stages of growth, then each season as a sheet of the plants, the same shore from close, and the same wood from the middle view. Everything is drawn by the one rule, so it is the same in both views.

Stages of growth

Four stages by biomass: a sprout (under 17 % of what a cell can hold), young (to 50 %), grown (to 85 %) and old, which for a tree is no taller than a grown one but broader and stouter. Until now there were two, young and grown, so a tree that had only just taken root looked like one half its age. In this world's woods half of the tree cells hold sprouts, a third young trees, and one in seven a grown or old one.

Broadleaf and conifer: sprout, young, grown, old. Then a bush the same; grass short, young and grown; dried grass; and what a fire leaves: a charred stump, two snags, a log.

Spring

Fresh, lighter greens. One broadleaf in three is a kind that flowers and two bushes in three are in blossom; conifers put out light tips; the grass is short; daisies and bells are out.

Summer

The colours you have seen so far. One bush in three is in berry; all four flowers are out; reeds are green.

Autumn

Broadleaves turn, each its own way: one in three gold, one flame, one still half green. Bushes go rust and two in three carry berries; grass yellows and goes to seed; ferns brown; mushrooms are at their most; few flowers are left.

Winter

Broadleaves and bushes are bare (a few berries stay on); conifers darken; grass is dried and pressed flat, as the simulation's own winter has low growth die back to its roots. No flowers, herbs or mushrooms.

Winter, under snow

The same winter with snow lying on it: on the crowns, the boughs, the stones, and on the ground in patches, thinner under trees. The simulation has no snow on low ground, so this one is the renderer's own to add or leave out.

6. What it costs

The board is not on the desk, so this is an estimate: the three views measured on the development PC and scaled to the board's processor. An Allwinner H618 scores about 150 in Geekbench 6 on one core and this PC about 3,150: the board is some 21 times slower per core. It is another architecture and another graphics stack, so read the last column as an order of magnitude.

What follows from it:

  • Drawing the frames fits in all three views, with room. The local view is the cheapest per frame: few animals are in sight.
  • Painting ground is the one thing that does not fit in a frame. On the board a piece of the local view's ground would stop the picture for half a second, and the daily repaint of the other two for eleven. Both have to be painted in the background (a worker), ahead of the camera for the local view. Measured as it is, the local view drops to 2 frames a second while a piece is painted.
  • Everything still hangs on the graphics driver. With the GPU the picture is small work; without it nothing here is fast enough. Reports for this board show the driver loading while Chromium still draws in software, so this is the first thing to try when the board is on the desk.
  • Memory fits. The board on the desk is the 2 GB model: Chromium's 0.6 GB and the 0.3 GB for simulation and server leave room.

Measured again with this sheet: painting the ground costs up to a fifth more than before (the middle view's now carries every plant's shadow), the frames the same. "At the board's speed" is the same page with its processor throttled 21 times in Chromium; in brackets, the slowest frame in a hundred. The page's process and the memory are Chromium's own figures on the PC. Sources for the scores: Geekbench 6 uploads for the Orange Pi Zero 3 (same chip) and for the Core Ultra 9 275HX.

To choose, and to say

  • Snow: whether winter should have it (the last season shown) or stay bare. The simulation has no snow on low ground, so it would be the renderer's own.
  • The seasons' colours: autumn and spring above all. Say what looks off or too much.
  • The stages: whether four is right, and whether an old tree should stand out more from a grown one.
  • Trunks and bushes: whether the bark and the mounds are what you had in mind.
