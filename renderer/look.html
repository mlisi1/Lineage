Lineage look sheet

How the renderer draws the world today: terrain, plants, creatures, and all three together. World: …. Renderer build ….

Everything here is drawn by the renderer spike (R0), the code the device benchmark runs. Pictures marked full size are 1024×600: pixel for pixel what the 7″ screen will show. The plant drawings and the creature bodies are a first pass, made to judge direction and cost; R1 and R2 replace them, so this sheet is the place to say what should change.

This page is live (drawn with …): the creatures walk, at many times the speed of the simulation. npm run look writes a still copy to reports/R0-look-sheet/.

A still copy, generated by npm run look in renderer/; do not edit by hand. For the moving version run npm run dev there and open /look.html.

1. The island from afar

A wide shot shows the whole world. One cell of the simulation is 4 screen pixels, so there is no room for drawings: the picture is a map.

2. Plants

From close up every vegetated cell gets a drawing, 8 art pixels wide: by form, and by growth (young below half biomass, grown above). Each kind has two drawings, picked by the cell's position, so a meadow is not wallpaper. Low plants that have died back are drawn in dry colours.

The drawings, six times enlarged. Colours are the report maps' own ramps.

3. Creatures

A creature is a chain of circles seen from above. The head moves; every segment follows the one in front at a fixed distance, which bends the body by itself. Legs plant their feet and step when the body has left them behind. On that chain goes whatever the genes ask for.

Fifteen specimens

The reference genomes the simulation is tested against, from a mouse to a giant. Each is followed by its own camera at the close-up's scale, then enlarged three times so the pixels can be read: on the screen itself they are a third of this size.

One gene at a time

The same plain animal with a single gene at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1, on plain sand (or sea, for those that live in it). Enlarged twice.

4. Together

One staged shot of a shore: the fifteen specimens and ten of the world's own herd, placed in view (swimmers in the water). It is drawn three times, with art pixels of 1, 2 and 4 screen pixels. That size is still to be chosen, and it decides both the look and how much the device has to draw.

Light

The time of day is one colour multiplied over the whole picture. Presence will drive it (R4); the four colours are placeholders.

Half size: the 512×300 drawing shown without stretching.

What is not settled

  • The size of an art pixel: 1, 2 or 4 screen pixels (section 4).
  • How close the camera gets. At the close-up used here a mid-sized animal is about 20 screen pixels long and a mouse about 11. Portraits like those of section 3 need a camera three times closer.
  • The ground in close-ups is one flat square per cell. Softer shorelines or detail inside a cell would be new work in R1.
  • Creature bodies are circles and stretched squares. Heads, shells and fins drawn as pixel art, and depth (an animal behind a tree), belong to R2.
  • Colour on the ground. A green animal on grass nearly disappears. That may be right (camouflage) or may need an outline.
