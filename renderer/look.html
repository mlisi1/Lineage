Lineage look sheet

How the renderer draws the world, and what there is to choose. World: …. Renderer build ….

The screen shows one of two views. The map view holds the whole island; the local view is four times closer and is where plants and animals are worth looking at. Both are painted from the same cells by the same rules, so the local view is the map with what lies between its cells worked out, not another picture.

Art pixels are 2 screen pixels, as settled with the painted ground: every picture marked full size is drawn at 512×300 and shown at 1024×600, pixel for pixel what the 7″ screen will show. Each section below changes one thing and holds the rest at what I would pick, which is marked. Everything is drawn by the renderer spike (R0); plants and animals are drawn by rule, so that sizes and styles can be compared, and can be redrawn by hand once chosen.

This page is live (drawn with …): the creatures walk, at many times the speed of the simulation. npm run look writes a still copy to reports/R0-look-sheet/.

3. The local view: plants

Grass was as big as a tree. Now grass and lichen are the ground's own texture, with only a small tuft here and there, and what stands is bushes and trees, at three sizes to choose from. The bigger they are, the fewer cells of a wood carry one, or a forest would be a single roof. Each plant has an outline and throws a shadow, and animals pass behind the ones in front of them.

P1. Small: a tree 12 pixels tall, one in every cell of a wood

6. What it costs

The board is not on the desk, so this is an estimate: the two views drawn as picked above, measured on the development PC and scaled to the board's processor. An Allwinner H618 scores about 150 in Geekbench 6 on one core and this PC about 3,150: the board is some 21 times slower per core. It is another architecture and another graphics stack, so read the last column as an order of magnitude.

On the PC At the board's speed Against
Script per frame, map view (216 animals as dots) 0.3 ms 3 ms (up to 8) 33 ms a frame at 30 fps
Script per frame, local view (50 animals) 0.3 ms 4 ms (up to 13)
Script per frame, local view (all 216 animals alive) 0.7 ms 12 to 15 ms (up to 26)
The page's process, local view 7 % of a core at 60 fps about 0.7 of a core at 30 fps 4 cores, of which the simulation may use ¼
Painting the ground (both views) 0.55 s 8 to 10 s once a sim-day; must not hold up frames
Pixels to fill per frame 512×300, two to three layers deep: about 0.4 million a Mali-G31 MP2, if Chromium is given it
WebGL drawn by the processor instead 2 to 3 cores out of reach 4 cores, 21 times slower
Chromium's memory 0.5 to 0.6 GB, plus 10 MB for the painted ground 1 to 4 GB by model; simulation and server get 0.3

What follows from it:

  • The page's own work fits, with room, as long as only the animals in sight are worked out (which is how these numbers were taken) and the map shows dots.
  • The ground cannot be repainted in one go on the board: ten seconds without a frame. It has to be painted in the background or a few cells per frame, which is easy to arrange since it changes once a sim-day.
  • Everything still hangs on the graphics driver. With the GPU the picture is small work; without it nothing here is fast enough. Reports for this board show the driver loading while Chromium still draws in software, so this is the first thing to try when the board is on the desk.
  • A 1 GB board is tight next to the simulation; 2 GB or more is safer.

"At the board's speed" is the same page with its processor throttled 20 to 24 times in Chromium; in brackets, the slowest frame in a hundred. The page's process and the memory are Chromium's own figures on the PC. Sources for the scores: Geekbench 6 uploads for the Orange Pi Zero 3 (same chip) and for the Core Ultra 9 275HX.
